Noise in the conflict-informed helper hcli

On the Gallagher functions of the experiments (D = 2), the helper hcli is built from the components of f: a valley on x* and a hill on every local optimum. Here you can add one type of error to its hills (or move its valley), at the levels used in the runs, and see the helper and its conflict with f. Conflict at a point is cos ∠(∇f, ∇h): red where going down in f means going up in h, blue where both go down together. The helper is designed to be blue in the basin of x* and red at the local optima.

f has 100 Gaussian components. Component 0 sits on x* with w0 = 10 and condition number α0 = 100; the 99 local weights are equally spaced over [1.1, 9.1] and the condition numbers αi of Ci are uniform in [50, 100]. L is the set of components whose centre is a local optimum of f. In hcli the valley w0 − w0g0 leads to x* and every local optimum of f becomes a hill.

The noise changes only the hills of hcli (the components of L), one type of error at a time; f and the valley g0 stay exact. The one exception is the valley shift of the further runs, which moves g0 and leaves the hills exact. Each noisy helper is the one of the runs: one random draw per function and setting, made with the code of the runs (random generator seeded with 1000 + the function's seed).

How the maps are computed

The page evaluates f and h itself, from the parameters of their components (centres, weights and the matrices Qi⊤CiQi / (2D)); no run data is loaded. The maps are drawn on a 300 × 300 grid of the window, with the nodes of numpy.linspace on both axes; on the whole box this is the grid of the shares printed in the paper. Gradients are exact (the gradient of the largest component). The colour of f and h is the rank of the value among the grid points of the panel, in 30 bands (0 lowest, 1 highest), so that each map uses the whole colour scale whatever its range. The cosine is drawn in 20 bands. The shares are computed on the drawn grid: when you zoom, they refer to the part of the basin and of the zones of the local optima inside the window.

Error models (one per type, applied to every hill): missing local optima, a share ρ of the hills is dropped at random; centre, a normal step with standard deviation εμ·si in each coordinate, si log-normal with σ = 0.25, clipped to the box; depth, wi times a log-normal factor with σ = εw, at most the largest local weight of f; elongation, αi times a log-normal factor with σ = εα, kept within [1, 1000], with the same long axis; rotation, a turn by a normal angle with standard deviation εθ (radians); valley shift, the centre of g0 moves x0 standard deviations of g0 away from x* in one random direction per function, clipped to the box. The larger levels (dashed buttons) come from further runs made to look for a threshold of acceptable error.
